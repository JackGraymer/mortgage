# Purchase Process

Buying property in Switzerland follows a well-defined sequence. The critical moment is the **notarial deed** — the purchase becomes legally binding when you sign at the notary's office.

```
Search → Reservation → Financing → Notarial deed → Land register → Handover
```

## Step 1: Property search

-   Browse listings on **immoscout24.ch**, **homegate.ch**, **comparis.ch** and local real-estate agents
-   Visit properties and attend open houses
-   Get a sense of market prices using the notary's published transaction records or online valuation tools

!!! tip
    Ask for the *Grundbuchauszug* (land register extract), floor plans and building-permit history before making an offer.

## Step 2: Reservation / letter of intent

-   Once you find a property, you may sign a **reservation agreement** or **letter of intent (LOI)**
-   A deposit (typically **5–10% of the purchase price**) is often required
-   The deposit is held in the **notary's escrow account**
-   **Important:** the reservation agreement is usually **binding** — ensure financing is secure first

!!! warning
    Do not sign a reservation before you have a mortgage pre-approval. Pulling out of a binding reservation can cost you the deposit.

## Step 3: Financing (mortgage pre-approval)

-   Obtain a **mortgage pre-approval** or firm commitment from your bank
-   This gives certainty about the amount, rate and terms
-   Pre-approval is typically valid for **3–6 months**

## Step 4: Notarial deed (Kaufvertrag)

-   The purchase contract is drafted by a **notary public** (Notar / notaire)
-   Both parties sign the deed at the notary's office
-   The notary verifies the legal status of the property (no liens, encumbrances, etc.)
-   **The deed is legally binding at the moment of signing**

## Step 5: Land register entry (Grundbuch)

-   The notary submits the deed to the **land registry** (Grundbuchamt)
-   The change of ownership is officially recorded
-   The mortgage is entered as a lien (*Schuldbrief*) on the property
-   This step finalizes the transfer of ownership

## Step 6: Handover

-   The keys are handed over
-   A **handover protocol** (Übergabeprotokoll / PV) documents the condition of the property
-   Utility accounts are transferred to the new owner

## Typical timeline

| Phase | Duration |
| ----- | -------- |
| Property search | 1–6 months |
| Reservation to notarial deed | 4–8 weeks |
| Notarial deed to land register | 2–4 weeks |
| Total (after finding a property) | 2–3 months |
