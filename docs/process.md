# Purchase Process

Buying property in Switzerland follows a well-defined sequence. The critical moment is the **notarial deed** — the purchase becomes legally binding when you sign at the notary's office.

```
Search → Financing → Reservation → Notarial deed → Land register → Handover
```

!!! note "Order matters"
    Get financing **before** signing a reservation. Many buyers get pre-approved first, then search, then reserve.

## Step 1: Financial preparation (before searching)

-   Check your budget: equity, affordability (33% rule), closing costs
-   Get a **mortgage pre-approval** from 2–3 lenders (valid 3–6 months)
-   Decide on Pillar 2/3a strategy (withdraw vs. pledge)
-   Gather documents: tax returns, salary certificates, asset statements, debt register extract (Betreibungsregisterauszug)

## Step 2: Property search

-   Browse listings on **immoscout24.ch**, **homegate.ch**, **comparis.ch**, **flatfox.ch** and local real-estate agents
-   Visit properties and attend open houses
-   Get a sense of market prices using the notary's published transaction records or online valuation tools (e.g., Wüest Partner, IAZI, bank tools)

!!! tip
    Ask for the *Grundbuchauszug* (land register extract), floor plans, building-permit history, and GEAK/CECB energy certificate before making an offer.

## Step 3: Reservation / letter of intent

-   Once you find a property, you may sign a **reservation agreement** (Reservationsvertrag) or **letter of intent (LOI)**
-   A deposit (typically **5–10% of the purchase price**) is often required
-   The deposit is held in the **notary's escrow account** (Notaranderkonto)
-   **Important:** the reservation agreement is usually **binding** — ensure financing is secure first

!!! warning
    Do not sign a reservation before you have a mortgage pre-approval. Pulling out of a binding reservation can cost you the deposit.

## Step 4: Notarial deed (Kaufvertrag / acte de vente)

-   The purchase contract is drafted by a **notary public** (Notar / notaire) — mandatory in all cantons
-   Both parties sign the deed at the notary's office
-   The notary verifies the legal status of the property (no liens, encumbrances, right of first refusal, etc.)
-   **The deed is legally binding at the moment of signing**
-   The notary also handles the transfer tax declaration and coordinates with the land registry

## Step 5: Land register entry (Grundbuch / registre foncier)

-   The notary submits the deed to the **land registry** (Grundbuchamt)
-   The change of ownership is officially recorded
-   The mortgage is entered as a lien (*Schuldbrief* / cédule hypothécaire) on the property
-   This step finalizes the transfer of ownership (takes 2–4 weeks)

## Step 6: Handover (Übergabe / remise)

-   The keys are handed over (usually on the land register entry date or agreed date)
-   A **handover protocol** (Übergabeprotokoll / procès-verbal de remise) documents the condition of the property, meter readings, defects
-   Utility accounts (electricity, gas, water, internet, waste) are transferred to the new owner
-   Building insurance (Gebäudeversicherung) is mandatory in all cantons — arrange transfer/new policy

## Typical timeline

| Phase | Duration |
| ----- | -------- |
| Financial preparation | 2–4 weeks |
| Property search | 1–6 months |
| Reservation to notarial deed | 3–6 weeks |
| Notarial deed to land register | 2–4 weeks |
| Handover | Same day as land register entry (typical) |
| **Total (after finding a property)** | **2–3 months** |

## Costs at each stage

| Stage | Typical costs (buyer) |
| ----- | --------------------- |
| Reservation | Deposit 5–10% (held in escrow) |
| Notarial deed | Notary fees 0.1–0.5%, transfer tax 0.1–3% (canton-dependent) |
| Land register | 0.1–0.3% |
| Mortgage setup | CHF 500–2,000 + valuation CHF 500–1,500 |
| Handover | Moving, utility deposits, insurance |
