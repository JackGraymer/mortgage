# Overview

Switzerland has one of the most stable real estate markets in Europe, driven by historically low interest rates, strong demand and a limited supply of building land. Home ownership rates are relatively low — around **40%** — compared with neighbouring countries, partly due to high property prices and strict financing requirements.

## Market characteristics

-   High purchase prices, especially in urban centres such as Zurich, Geneva and Basel
-   Strict mortgage lending criteria and conservative affordability rules
-   Large cantonal variation in taxes, fees and procedures
-   A well-regulated banking system with strong consumer protections

!!! info "Focus of this guide"
    This guide focuses on the cantons of **Zurich (ZH)** and **Aargau (AG)**. The underlying rules are largely national, but every canton sets its own tax rates, fees and parts of the purchase procedure.

## Why the market behaves the way it does

Several structural factors keep Swiss property prices high and ownership rates low:

-   **Scarce buildable land.** Switzerland is mountainous, and strict zoning keeps the supply of developable land tight, particularly in the urban belt (Zurich, Zug, the Lake Geneva arc, Basel).
-   **High financing hurdles.** Banks apply a conservative affordability test (see [Financing & Mortgages](financing/index.md)) that effectively caps the affordable purchase price at around **5 to 6 times** gross household income.
-   **Safe-haven demand.** Political stability, the strong franc and historically low rates have attracted both domestic and international investment demand.
-   **Renting is the norm.** Around 60% of households rent, partly because the tax system historically favoured renters, and because high prices make ownership a long-term commitment.

## Interest-rate environment

Swiss mortgage rates are anchored to the Swiss National Bank (SNB) policy rate and the **SARON** benchmark. Key facts:

-   After a long period of near-zero policy rates (2015–2022), the SNB raised rates through 2023, peaking around 1.75%, then started cutting again in 2024.
-   Fixed-rate mortgages (2, 5, 10 or 15 years) are priced off long-term swap rates and are the most common choice.
-   SARON mortgages track the overnight benchmark and are usually cheaper at the start but carry interest-rate risk.

!!! tip
    For personal planning, a **fixed-rate mortgage** gives predictable costs. Banks stress-test your finances at an assumed **5% interest rate** regardless of the actual rate you pay — see the affordability rules in the next section.

## Regional differences

| Region | Typical characteristics |
| ------ | ----------------------- |
| **Zurich (ZH)** | High prices, strong demand, deep mortgage market (ZKB, banks, brokers), higher tax multiplier but high wages. |
| **Aargau (AG)** | More affordable than Zurich, good access to the A1/A3 corridors, moderate tax burden. |
| **Lake Geneva arc** | Among the most expensive areas in the country (Geneva, Lausanne). |
| **Ticino, Valais** | Lower prices; holiday-home rules (Lex Koller tourist zones) apply in places. |
