# Financing & Mortgages

Swiss mortgage lending follows a strict, conservative model. Understanding the two-pillar structure, the equity rules and the affordability test is the most important part of planning a purchase.

!!! tip "Skip to the calculator"
    If you already know the numbers, use the [Amortization Calculator](calculator.md) to see the interest, principal and monthly costs for your situation.

## The two-pillar mortgage structure

Swiss mortgages are split into two "pillars":

| Pillar | % of property value | Characteristics |
| ------ | ------------------- | --------------- |
| **1st mortgage** | Up to 65–67% | No amortization required (interest-only) |
| **2nd mortgage** | Up to 12–15% | Must be amortized within 15–20 years or by retirement age (65) |

The **total loan-to-value (LTV)** cannot exceed **80%** of the property value.

!!! note "Why the 1st mortgage is interest-only"
    The 1st mortgage (the bottom 65%) is typically **never amortized** — you pay interest on it for as long as you own the property. Only the 2nd mortgage (the top part between 65% and 80%) is paid down, usually over 15 years. Many owners pay off the 2nd mortgage and then hold the property interest-only.

## Equity requirements

-   **Minimum equity:** 20% of the property value
-   **Hard cash equity:** at least **10%** of the property value must come from liquid assets (cash, securities). Pension fund withdrawals (Pillar 2) or Pillar 3a withdrawals **cannot** cover this "hard" portion
-   The remaining 10% can be financed via Pillar 2 (pension fund advance or pledge) or a Pillar 3a withdrawal

**Example — property value CHF 1,000,000:**

| Source | Amount |
| ------ | ------ |
| Hard cash (10%) | CHF 100,000 |
| Pillar 2 or 3a (10%) | CHF 100,000 |
| 1st mortgage (65%) | CHF 650,000 |
| 2nd mortgage (15%) | CHF 150,000 |

## Affordability calculation

Banks apply a strict **affordability test**: total housing costs must not exceed **33% of gross household income**.

**Housing costs considered:**

-   Imputed mortgage interest at a **stress rate** (typically **5%**, even if the actual rate is lower)
-   Amortization of the 2nd mortgage (1% of the 2nd mortgage amount per year, or the actual schedule)
-   Maintenance costs (typically 1% of the property value per year)
-   Heating and ancillary costs

**Example — property CHF 1,000,000, 1st CHF 650k @ 5% stress, 2nd CHF 150k:**

| Cost item | Annual amount |
| --------- | ------------- |
| Interest (stress 5% on CHF 650k) | CHF 32,500 |
| Amortization (1% of CHF 150k) | CHF 1,500 |
| Maintenance (1% of property value) | CHF 10,000 |
| Ancillary costs (estimated) | CHF 3,000 |
| **Total** | **CHF 47,000** |

Required minimum income at the 33% ratio: **CHF 142,400 / year**.

!!! warning
    The stress test is the real constraint for most buyers. Even at a low actual rate of 2%, the bank tests you at **5%**. A rough rule of thumb: you can typically afford a property worth about **5–6× your gross annual income**.

## Interest-rate types

| Type | Description |
| ---- | ----------- |
| **Fixed-rate mortgage** | Fixed interest for a defined term (2, 5, 10, 15 years). Predictable costs. |
| **SARON mortgage** | Variable rate based on the Swiss Overnight Repo Average. Cheaper initially, subject to fluctuations. |
| **Variable mortgage** | Rate set by the bank, can change at any time. Flexible but riskier. |

!!! tip
    For a primary residence, a **fixed-rate mortgage** removes the biggest financial risk (rising rates). SARON makes sense when you expect rates to fall or stay flat.

## Using Pillar 2 and Pillar 3a

**Pillar 2 (pension fund / BVG):**

-   You can either **withdraw** funds (to buy a principal residence) or **pledge** your pension account as collateral
-   Withdrawal is tax-free at the time, but is taxed when the property is sold or when you retire
-   An advance withdrawal reduces your future retirement benefits
-   Both spouses' Pillar 2 savings can be used

**Pillar 3a (vested retirement savings):**

-   Can be fully withdrawn for the purchase of a principal residence
-   The withdrawal is taxed separately at a reduced rate
-   Can also be pledged as collateral

!!! warning "Think before withdrawing pension money"
    Withdrawing Pillar 2 money reduces your retirement capital permanently, and the tax on a later sale can be substantial. Pledging instead of withdrawing often preserves your retirement savings — discuss this with a specialist.
