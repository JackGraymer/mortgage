# Visiting & Inspecting Properties

The visit is your main due-diligence tool. Residential property in Switzerland is generally sold **as seen** ("as is"): the seller guarantees only what is written in the purchase contract, and hidden defects become your problem once the deed is signed. A systematic approach — homework *before* you visit and a disciplined inspection *during* it — is the cheapest insurance you can buy.

!!! tip
    Keep [the viewing scorecard](scorecard.md) open while reviewing candidates, and use the [interactive checklist](../checklist.md) to track the whole purchase end to end.

## 1. The go / no-go gate — homework before you visit

A 15-minute desktop check can stop you from wasting an afternoon on the wrong house — or from discovering the show-stopper only *after* signing.

### Documents to request before the visit

Ask the seller / agent for these before agreeing to a viewing:

| Document | What it tells you |
| -------- | ----------------- |
| **Grundbuchauszug** (land register extract) | Current owner, registered mortgage liens (*Schuldbriefe*), **easements** (*Dienstbarkeiten*: access, view, pipeline rights), annotations (options, pre-emption rights). A road or pipeline running through "your" garden can kill the deal cheaply on paper. |
| **GEAK/CECB energy certificate** | Official energy grade (A–G) plus recommended improvements. Must be shown when selling in many cantons — ask for it up front. |
| **Heating & hot-water bills, last 2–3 years** (oil orders, gas invoices, kWh) | The single most revealing number: actual running costs. Two visually similar houses can differ by **CHF 3,000–6,000/yr** in energy alone. |
| **Building permits & plans** (Baugesuche, Baubewilligung, Bauabnahme) | What was approved. Compare the actual floor plan with the permits during the visit. |
| **Renovation receipts** | Dates and invoices for roof, windows, heating, bathroom, kitchen, insulation, solar. A documented new roof beats a vague "recently renovated". |
| **Building insurance (Gebäudeversicherung) premium** | Mandatory cover; shows the insurer and typical cost. Note: in cantons with *private* building insurance (e.g. Aargau) you must arrange a new policy yourself. |
| **If rented:** tenant situation & notice period | A tenanted house cannot be delivered empty — clarify what you are actually buying. |

!!! warning "If they dodge"
    If the agent won't share the Grundbuchauszug, the energy bills or the permits, ask why. Genuine sellers hand these over without fuss. Reluctance is a signal.

### Desktop checks (free, online, 5–10 min each)

-   **Zoning:** *Zonenplan* + *Bau- und Zonenordnung* (BZO) on the municipality's GIS — what is the plot allowed to be? Building lines (*Grenz-/Bauabstände*), protected/inventory status (*Denkmalschutz*), minimum plot rules if you ever want to extend.
-   **Hazard maps:** flood, rockfall, landslide, avalanche (cantonal hazard GIS); groundwater protection zones.
-   **Noise:** street category, rail/aircraft noise maps (BAFU sonBASE); regional-airport flight paths.
-   **Radon:** cantonal radon maps for ground-floor / basement living rooms.
-   **Planned construction nearby:** municipal development plans — an apartment block may be coming across the street.
-   **Price sanity:** listing age and price reductions, comparable asking and sold prices, village price per m² (portals, bank/aggregator reports). A price 10% under the street is either a bargain or a red flag — find out which.
-   **Access & commute:** tram/bus/rail, parking, carsharing, schools, shops.

### Know the year, know the risks

| Built | What to expect | Budget implication |
| ----- | -------------- | ------------------ |
| **Before 1960** | Solid masonry; usually original single glazing, oil heating, sometimes lead pipes; woodworm possible in attics; no insulation | Replacement windows, possible lead pipe work, insulation retrofits |
| **1960–1985** | The **asbestos era**: vinyl floor tiles, *Eternit* corrugated roof sheets, sprayed insulation, pipe insulation; oil heating; poor insulation; often original electrical | Asbestos abatement + heating + window replacement |
| **1986–1995** | Asbestos banned for new construction in 1990 but older stock lingers; better insulation, metal/alu frames, first heat pumps | Scattered upgrades; check electrical panel age |
| **1996–2010** | Double glazing standard, decent insulation, heat pumps / modern gas frequent; early façade systems | Occasional facade (EIFS) issues |
| **After 2010** | Minergie era: efficient envelope, high certificate grades | Expensive to buy, cheap to run |

!!! note "Asbestos in a nutshell"
    If built before 1990, assume some asbestos exists (flooring, roof sheets, pipe and spray insulation). It is only dangerous when disturbed or removed carelessly — and removal costs money. Budget it before you plan any renovation.

### The financial gate before the visit

Run the [Affordability Calculator](../financing/affordability.md) with the asking price before you go. If your income can't pass the 5% stress test at that price, don't burn a visit. Also check:

-   **LTV**: (price − equity) ÷ price ≤ 80%.
-   **Closing costs** of 1–4% on top (see [Costs & Fees](../costs.md)).
-   **Renovation reserve** of ~1% of value per year plus any era-specific upgrades above.

### Questions to ask the agent before visiting (script)

1.  How long has it been listed? Any price reductions or serious offers so far?
2.  Why is the seller selling — and what is the timeline (is there a new-build waiting)?
3.  Year built; any extensions or major renovations since, with dates?
4.  Heating system type, age, last service; oil tank age/condition?
5.  May we see the heating/hot-water bills for the last 2–3 years and the GEAK certificate?
6.  Any known issues — moisture, mould, water damage, cracks, settlement, pests?
7.  Any third-party rights (easements) affecting the plot or buildings?
8.  What exactly is included in the price: kitchen, appliances, shutters/blinds, lighting, garden shed?
9.  Tenants? When is the property actually deliverable?
10. Are the building documents and Grundbuchauszug available for review?

## 2. During the visit — inspect with a system

Bring: torch, 5 m tape measure, notebook (plus a printed floor plan to annotate), phone (**ask before photographing**), and good shoes. Plan 45–90 minutes. Go around the outside first, then in.

### Outside first

-   **Roof:** age, material and condition; slipped, cracked or mossy tiles; gutters rusted/sagging/blocked; downpipes; chimney and flashings. For flat roofs ask when the membrane was last replaced (life ≈ 15–25 years). Confirm from the attic as well.
-   **Facade:** cracks or bulging plaster, damp bottom edges, algae/moss on north-west sides; ask what insulation exists and how thick. Balconies/terraces: membrane age, blisters; railings rust (*Spiankon* repairs are pricey).
-   **Windows (both sides):** single/double/triple glazing; frames — wood (rotting at the bottom?), aluminium, PVC; seals; hardware that opens/locks smoothly; shutters/blinds (*Storen*) function, incl. motors.
-   **Ground:** does the ground slope away from the walls? Any puddles? Retaining walls; trees or hedges touching the house; firewood stacked against the wall (moisture trap); drainage.
-   **Plot:** garden orientation and where the sun actually lands; how much flat usable area; boundaries vs. the Grundbuch; shared maintenance of access road/path.
-   **Feel the street:** stand outside for five minutes — traffic, trams, rail, neighbours.

### Inside — use your senses

-   **Smell (cheap, and very telling):** musty in the cellar, damp behind the kitchen, stale smoke, sewage. Be suspicious of a strong "fresh paint" smell mid-visit — it is the classic way damp and mould get hidden. Check dark corners, under window sills, behind furniture (ask before moving), inside cupboards and under kitchen sinks.
-   **Walls & ceilings:** hairline cracks are normal; diagonal or stepped cracks are not. Look for water stains, bulging plaster, flaking paint, efflorescence in the cellar.
-   **Cellar:** damp patches, musty air, bubbling paint, a sump pump (is it working?), ventilation, and — tellingly — whether anything is stored directly on the floor.
-   **Attic / roof space:** insulation type and thickness, ventilation, condensation or rust on nails, water/rot stains on beams, usable headroom. Look for woodworm/beetle holes (*Holzwurm*) in beams.
-   **Heating room:** boiler type and age (energy label), service stickers, oil tank age and corrosion (smell the vent), heat-pump unit condition, controls. Ask how the system is serviced.
-   **Hot water:** boiler/buffer age; solar thermal collectors?
-   **Electrical:** fuse box — modern breakers (FI/RCD) are the current norm; wiring age; socket count and placement (home office? EV charger?); capacity for a heat pump or solar.
-   **Plumbing:** run every tap (pressure), flush every toilet, watch how fast drains empty, look under sinks for leaks and rust.
-   **Kitchen & bathrooms:** age of fixtures, grout condition, ventilation/extractor function.
-   **Layout & feel:** tape-measure the rooms; open and close every door and window; stairs and handrail; storage (built-ins, attics); which rooms get sun at which time; mobile signal in each room (relevant for home office).
-   **What stays:** agree in writing what is included — kitchen, built-in wardrobes, AV equipment, garden-shed contents, garage.

### Red flags — negotiate or walk away

-   Diagonal/stepped cracks, or doors/windows that suddenly jam (possible settlement)
-   Musty cellar, mould behind furniture, or suspiciously fresh paint covering walls
-   Water stains on ceilings under bathrooms or roof edges
-   Old, corroded, smelly **oil tank** — a leak means contaminated soil and cleanup costs
-   Extensions, bathrooms or attic conversions that look unauthorised — ask for the permits
-   Seller deflects on bills, documents or direct questions
-   Suspiciously low price (easements, heritage protection, soil pollution, leasehold)
-   Long-vacant house or blanket "as is, no discussion" conditions
-   No insulation + oil heating on an older house — that is your future bill
-   Radon hotspot with an unventilated basement living area

### Positives worth money

-   Roof, windows or heating recently replaced — receipts prove it
-   Dry, aired cellar; an organised seller with plans and receipts
-   Heat pump / pellets / modern gas, good GEAK grade, Minergie traces
-   South-facing garden, flat plot, own parking/garage
-   Low energy bills and low insurance premium
-   Straight walls, even floors, quality windows, quiet surroundings
-   Extension potential (enough side distances, permissive zoning) — builds long-term value

## 3. After the visit — decide like a buyer

-   **Score it cold** in the [Viewing Scorecard](scorecard.md) right away — impressions fade and candidates blur together after the third house.
-   Use the **24-hour rule**: sleep on it. Plan a **second visit in the evening or on a weekend** to test light and noise, and ideally in rain to see how the house sheds water.
-   Request whatever paperwork is still missing and get the inclusion list confirmed in writing.
-   For anything **pre-1980** or any doubt, commission an independent inspection (engineer, SVIT expert or GEAK advisor — ~CHF 500–1,500). It is routinely the best money of the entire purchase.
-   Re-run your numbers with real inputs: [Amortization Calculator](../financing/calculator.md), [Affordability Calculator](../financing/affordability.md), [Tax Estimator](../taxation/calculator.md).
-   Use documented defects as negotiation leverage (*Preisabzug*) — but in hot markets be ready to act fast; don't lose the house over 1%.
-   After signing a reservation you typically get a **10–14 day protection window**: order the bank valuation, the final Grundbuchauszug, the fire-insurance certificate and verify tenancy *before* the notarial deed. See [Purchase Process](../process.md).

### Rapid decision table

| Finding | Action |
| ------- | ------ |
| Moisture stains or damp smell | Ask for damage history + insurance claims; order an inspection |
| Oil heating, any age | Ask for bills; assume replacement CHF 25,000–40,000 within 5–10 years |
| Single glazing (pre-2000) | Budget windows CHF 15,000–25,000+ |
| Eternit / asbestos materials | Budget removal; never DIY |
| Easement restricting you | Check it is lived-with-able on paper before negotiating |
| Roof or flat-roof membrane > 15–20 yr | Budget CHF 20,000–40,000 |
| Unauthorised extension | Verify it can be regularised (Baubewilligung) or price the risk |
| Price 10%+ under the street | Find out why — in writing — before getting excited |
