# Before the Visit

Residential property in Switzerland is generally sold **as seen** ("as is"): the seller guarantees only what is written in the purchase contract, and hidden defects become your problem once the deed is signed. The cheapest insurance you can buy is homework — a 15-minute desktop check can stop you from wasting an afternoon on the wrong house, or from discovering the show-stopper only *after* signing.

!!! tip
    Keep [the viewing scorecard](scorecard.md) open while reviewing candidates, and use the [interactive checklist](../checklist.md) to track the whole purchase end to end. When you go, use the [During the Visit](during.md) inspection system; afterwards, [decide like a buyer](after.md).

## Documents to request before the visit

Ask the seller / agent for these before agreeing to a viewing:

| Document | What it tells you |
| -------- | ----------------- |
| **Grundbuchauszug** (land register extract) | Current owner, registered mortgage liens (*Schuldbriefe*), **easements** (*Dienstbarkeiten*: access, view, pipeline rights), annotations (options, pre-emption rights). A road or pipeline running through "your" garden can kill the deal cheaply on paper. |
| **GEAK/CECB energy certificate** | Official energy grade (A–G) plus recommended improvements. Must be shown when selling in many cantons — ask for it up front. |
| **Heating & hot-water bills, last 2–3 years** (oil orders, gas invoices, kWh) | The single most revealing number: actual running costs. Two visually similar houses can differ by **CHF 3,000–6,000/yr** in energy alone. |
| **Building permits & plans** (Baugesuche, Baubewilligung, Bauabnahme) | What was approved. Compare the actual floor plan with the permits during the visit. |
| **Renovation receipts** | Dates and invoices for roof, windows, heating, bathroom, kitchen, insulation, solar. A documented new roof beats a vague "recently renovated". |
| **Building insurance (Gebäudeversicherung) premium** | Mandatory cover; shows the insurer and typical cost. Note: in cantons with *private* building insurance (e.g. Aargau) you must arrange a new policy yourself. |
| **If rented:** tenant situation & notice period | A tenanted house cannot be delivered empty — clarify what you are actually buying. |

!!! warning "If they dodge"
    If the agent won't share the Grundbuchauszug, the energy bills or the permits, ask why. Genuine sellers hand these over without fuss. Reluctance is a signal.

## Desktop checks (free, online, 5–10 min each)

-   **Zoning:** *Zonenplan* + *Bau- und Zonenordnung* (BZO) on the municipality's GIS — what is the plot allowed to be? Building lines (*Grenz-/Bauabstände*), protected/inventory status (*Denkmalschutz*), minimum plot rules if you ever want to extend.
-   **Hazard maps:** flood, rockfall, landslide, avalanche (cantonal hazard GIS); groundwater protection zones.
-   **Noise:** street category, rail/aircraft noise maps (BAFU sonBASE); regional-airport flight paths.
-   **Radon:** cantonal radon maps for ground-floor / basement living rooms.
-   **Planned construction nearby:** municipal development plans — an apartment block may be coming across the street.
-   **Price sanity:** listing age and price reductions, comparable asking and sold prices, village price per m² (portals, bank/aggregator reports). A price 10% under the street is either a bargain or a red flag — find out which.
-   **Access & commute:** tram/bus/rail, parking, carsharing, schools, shops.

## Know the year, know the risks

| Built | What to expect | Budget implication |
| ----- | -------------- | ------------------ |
| **Before 1960** | Solid masonry; usually original single glazing, oil heating, sometimes lead pipes; woodworm possible in attics; no insulation | Replacement windows, possible lead pipe work, insulation retrofits |
| **1960–1985** | The **asbestos era**: vinyl floor tiles, *Eternit* corrugated roof sheets, sprayed insulation, pipe insulation; oil heating; poor insulation; often original electrical | Asbestos abatement + heating + window replacement |
| **1986–1995** | Asbestos banned for new construction in 1990 but older stock lingers; better insulation, metal/alu frames, first heat pumps | Scattered upgrades; check electrical panel age |
| **1996–2010** | Double glazing standard, decent insulation, heat pumps / modern gas frequent; early façade systems | Occasional facade (EIFS) issues |
| **After 2010** | Minergie era: efficient envelope, high certificate grades | Expensive to buy, cheap to run |

!!! note "Asbestos in a nutshell"
    If built before 1990, assume some asbestos exists (flooring, roof sheets, pipe and spray insulation). It is only dangerous when disturbed or removed carelessly — and removal costs money. Budget it before you plan any renovation.

## The financial gate before the visit

Run the [Affordability Calculator](../financing/affordability.md) with the asking price before you go. If your income can't pass the 5% stress test at that price, don't burn a visit. Also check:

-   **LTV**: (price − equity) ÷ price ≤ 80%.
-   **Closing costs** of 1–4% on top (see [Costs & Fees](../costs.md)).
-   **Renovation reserve** of ~1% of value per year plus any era-specific upgrades above.

## Questions to ask the agent before visiting (script)

1.  How long has it been listed? Any price reductions or serious offers so far?
2.  Why is the seller selling — and what is the timeline (is there a new-build waiting)?
3.  Year built; any extensions or major renovations since, with dates?
4.  Heating system type, age, last service; oil tank age/condition?
5.  May we see the heating/hot-water bills for the last 2–3 years and the GEAK certificate?
6.  Any known issues — moisture, mould, water damage, cracks, settlement, pests?
7.  Any third-party rights (easements) affecting the plot or buildings?
8.  What exactly is included in the price: kitchen, appliances, shutters/blinds, lighting, garden shed?
9.  Tenants? When is the property actually deliverable?
10. Are the building documents and Grundbuchauszug available for review?
